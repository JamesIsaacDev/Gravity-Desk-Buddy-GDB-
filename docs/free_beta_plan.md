\# Gravity Desk Buddy (GDB) — Free Beta Plan



\## Purpose



The first GDB beta is designed to test whether the \*\*core Focus + Buddy experience\*\* creates enough real value that users want to return.



The beta is not meant to prove that GDB is finished, validate every future feature, or maximize growth. It is meant to reduce uncertainty around the foundation.



The core question is:



> \\\*\\\*Does GDB help users do meaningful work, feel supported while doing it, and want to come back?\\\*\\\*



The first beta should help identify:

\- whether users actually return,

\- whether the Buddy adds meaningful value,

\- whether the focus experience feels useful rather than interchangeable,

\- what feels missing,

\- what feels unnecessary,

\- and what should change before the next cohort.



\## Beta Hypothesis



GDB may create value by combining focused work with a lightweight digital companion that helps users feel supported, accountable, and connected to their progress.



The first beta will test whether this core loop is strong enough to stand on its own:



\*\*Choose meaningful work → Start a focus session → Buddy supports the experience → Complete the session → See progress → Return later\*\*



The Buddy should support the user's mission without becoming the mission itself.



\## Tester Profile



\### Initial Cohort Size

Approximately \*\*10–20 external testers\*\*.



\### Ideal Tester Traits

The first testers should be people who:

\- value productivity, learning, creative work, or personal growth,

\- regularly perform self-directed work,

\- often study, build, create, or improve on their own,

\- want greater consistency or accountability,

\- are open to the idea of a digital productivity companion,

\- are willing to use the product repeatedly,

\- and can give candid feedback.



Potential backgrounds may include students, developers, creatives, founders, and other self-directed learners or builders.



The behavioral profile matters more than job title.



\### Who Should Not Be the Primary Cohort

Avoid relying primarily on:

\- close family and friends,

\- users testing only out of personal loyalty,

\- users who only want a basic Pomodoro timer,

\- people with little interest in productivity or self-directed work,

\- and users primarily looking for an AI relationship substitute.



\## Cohort Structure



\### Duration

\*\*14 days\*\*



\### Cohort 1 Flow

1\. Recruit approximately 10–20 suitable external testers.

2\. Give them access to the beta.

3\. Allow repeated use across two weeks.

4\. Collect behavioral data.

5\. Collect first-use and end-of-beta qualitative feedback.

6\. Review recurring patterns.

7\. Decide what should be refined, redesigned, added, removed, or deferred.

8\. Use the findings to shape Cohort 2.



The end of the 14-day period closes the \*\*first test cycle\*\*, not necessarily access to GDB forever.



If demand exceeds the planned cohort size, additional users can be waitlisted, admitted in later batches, or invited into a later cohort.



\## What Testers Will Test



Cohort 1 should focus on the smallest meaningful GDB foundation.



\### Core Experience

\- Starting and completing focus sessions

\- Returning to GDB across multiple days

\- Interacting with the Buddy in a lightweight way

\- Experiencing simple progress or continuity

\- Evaluating whether GDB helps them stay on track



\### Optional / Limited

Light reflection or journaling may be included if it supports the core loop without making the test too broad.



Heavy journaling is not required for Cohort 1.



\## Buddy Boundaries



\### The Buddy Should

\- interact with the user regularly,

\- ideally provide light daily presence,

\- acknowledge focus activity and completion,

\- provide general encouragement,

\- reinforce progress,

\- make focused work feel warmer and less solitary,

\- develop a recognizable visual identity,

\- and support the user's goals without taking over the experience.



\### The Buddy Should Not Yet

\- become deeply personalized,

\- make strong psychological interpretations,

\- create emotional obligation,

\- pressure the user to return,

\- over-message,

\- distract from focused work,

\- behave like a substitute for a human relationship,

\- or become more engaging than the work itself.



\### Product Principle

> \\\*\\\*Companion, not substitute. Encouragement, not dependency. Presence, not obligation.\\\*\\\*



The Buddy should reinforce the user's intended behavior rather than compete with it.



\## Behavioral Metrics



Track:

\- number of testers who activate at least once,

\- number who return on a different day,

\- active days per tester,

\- completed focus sessions,

\- average focus sessions per active tester,

\- incomplete or abandoned sessions,

\- return behavior after the first few days,

\- and whether usage declines, stays flat, or grows.



If journaling is included, track journal frequency and whether it appears to support return behavior.



\### Key Retention Signal

Distinguish between users who return because they \*\*want to\*\* and users who only return because they are reminded.



\*\*Unprompted return behavior\*\* is more meaningful than raw session count alone.



\## Qualitative Feedback Questions



\### After First Use

1\. What did you understand GDB to be after your first session?

2\. Did GDB help you start, stay with, or finish work you intended to do?

3\. How did the Buddy feel during the experience?

4\. Was anything confusing, awkward, distracting, or unnecessary?

5\. What felt missing after your first use?



\### After Repeated Use / End of Beta

1\. Did GDB help you get meaningful work done during the two weeks?

2\. At the end of a session or day, did you feel more satisfied with the work you completed?

3\. Did the Buddy make the experience feel more supported or accompanied?

4\. Did the Buddy ever feel distracting, forced, or gimmicky?

5\. What made you come back, if anything?

6\. Would you choose to use GDB again without being reminded? Why or why not?

7\. What felt missing?

8\. What felt unnecessary?

9\. What is one thing you would change?

10\. What would make you want to use GDB for another month?

11\. Would you want to see another version of GDB? Why?



The deeper question is:



> \\\*\\\*Did GDB help you feel more satisfied with the meaningful work you actually did, while giving you a sense that something was alongside you in the process?\\\*\\\*



\## Success Signals



Encouraging signs include:

\- testers say GDB helped them start or finish meaningful work,

\- testers return across multiple days without being chased,

\- users feel more satisfied with the work they completed,

\- the Buddy adds a sense of support, continuity, or companionship,

\- some testers mention the Buddy positively without being prompted,

\- users express curiosity about future versions,

\- and feedback becomes specific enough to guide the next iteration.



A particularly strong signal would be:



> \\\*\\\*“This helped me get work done, and I want to keep using it.”\\\*\\\*



\## Failure Signals



Take seriously:

\- most testers use GDB once and do not return,

\- the timer feels interchangeable with any basic focus timer,

\- the Buddy feels gimmicky,

\- the Buddy distracts from the work,

\- users understand the idea but do not enjoy the actual product,

\- users cannot explain what value GDB gave them,

\- testers need repeated reminders to return,

\- or users say they do not see a reason to open GDB again.



One especially important warning is:



> \\\*\\\*“I get what you're trying to do, but I don't feel a reason to open it again.”\\\*\\\*



\## Product Feedback Filtering



Every suggestion should be filtered through:



\### 1. Repeated Signal

Several testers independently identify the same need, pain point, or opportunity.



\### 2. Strategic Fit

The suggestion fits GDB's direction and strengthens the core experience.



\### 3. Noise

A one-off idea that may be interesting but does not fit current priorities.



The goal is not to implement every request. The goal is to identify \*\*patterns\*\*.



\## End-of-Cohort Review Gate



At the end of the 14-day test, review:

\- What did users actually do?

\- What did they say?

\- What repeated?

\- What failed?

\- What surprised us?

\- What made people return?

\- What made people stop?

\- What felt missing?

\- What was unnecessary?

\- What should change before Cohort 2?



> \\\*\\\*The first beta is expected to produce product changes. Success does not mean “no redesign needed.” Success means the feedback is clear enough to guide the next iteration.\\\*\\\*



\## Expansion vs. Redesign Logic



The decision after Cohort 1 should not be:



\*\*Good beta → expand\*\*

\*\*Bad beta → redesign\*\*



A better model is:



\*\*Beta Cohort 1 → collect evidence → refine or redesign deliberately → Cohort 2\*\*



If users return, complete meaningful work, value the Buddy, and ask for more, the next iteration may focus on refinement.



If users do not return, find the Buddy gimmicky, see no value beyond a timer, or cannot explain why they would use GDB again, the core loop may require deeper redesign.



\## Explicitly Out of Scope for Cohort 1



\- full Orbit progression systems,

\- social or community features,

\- payments,

\- premium tiers,

\- deep AI personalization,

\- emotionally dependent or relationship-substitute behavior,

\- heavy journaling systems,

\- advanced analytics for users,

\- referral or viral mechanics,

\- large-scale onboarding systems,

\- complex social identity systems,

\- and major features added solely because one tester requests them.



\## Later Cohorts and Orbit Testing



Orbits are important enough that they should not be casually added to Cohort 1.



\### Cohort 1

Prove the base foundation:

\- focus,

\- Buddy,

\- simple progress,

\- optional light reflection.



\### Cohort 2 or 3

Potentially test:

\- expanded journaling,

\- richer Buddy interactions,

\- early development mechanics,

\- or limited Orbit concepts.



\### Later

Test the full Orbit architecture, progression, identity, developmental systems, social/community implications, and any premium structure connected to it.



> \\\*\\\*Cohort 1 tests the foundation. Orbits should be tested separately once the foundation produces enough evidence to support them.\\\*\\\*

Prioritize \*\*fit and candor\*\* over raw volume.

\## Founder Mission and Product Principles



GDB is intended to help people make meaningful progress while feeling supported through the process.



A 14-day beta cannot prove that GDB is “life-changing.” It can look for early evidence that users:

\- get meaningful work done,

\- feel more supported while doing it,

\- feel more satisfied with their progress,

\- and want to return.



\### Core Principles

> \\\*\\\*The Buddy supports the mission; it does not become the mission.\\\*\\\*



> \\\*\\\*Companion, not substitute.\\\*\\\*



> \\\*\\\*Progress should feel supported, not pressured.\\\*\\\*



> \\\*\\\*Beta feedback informs the vision; it does not replace founder judgment.\\\*\\\*



> \\\*\\\*The goal is not to prove GDB is perfect. The goal is to learn what makes it worth returning to.\\\*\\\*
