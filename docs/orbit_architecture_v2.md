# GDB Orbit Architecture — Version 2.1

## Status

Current long-term Orbit architecture.

This document defines the philosophical and architectural direction of the
Gravity Desk Buddy Orbit developmental system.

It does not mean every component described here belongs in the MVP.

The current product remains centered on:

Focus
→ Buddy
→ Streaks / Consistency
→ Reflection
→ Basic Orbit representation

Orbit intelligence should become deeper only after real user behavior provides
evidence that the underlying concepts are useful.

Version 2.1 clarifies:

- the universal role of Parallax,
- progression philosophy,
- anti-hierarchy principles,
- cross-Orbit community design,
- private developmental evidence,
- the intentionally undefined progression mechanism,
- and the relationship between Orbit identity and the broader GDB experience.


---

## 1. Purpose

The Orbit system is GDB's long-term developmental layer.

Daily focus, reflection, consistency, Buddy interaction, and other meaningful
signals may create evidence over time.

V2 explores whether that accumulated evidence can help users better understand:

- how they work,
- how they learn,
- how they respond to difficulty,
- how consistently they act on intentions,
- how their judgment changes,
- how their interests and capabilities develop,
- how their reasoning develops,
- and how their behavior changes within a chosen domain.

The system should help users understand development rather than merely record
activity.

Orbits should make development more visible.

They should not reduce a person to a rank.


---

## 2. Core Question

GDB does not primarily ask:

> What do you do?

It asks:

> Who are you becoming, and what evidence demonstrates that development?

The answer should emerge from longitudinal evidence rather than one isolated
event, score, test, or opinion.


---

## 3. Orbit Philosophy

Orbits represent earned developmental placement within GDB over time.

They are NOT:

- purchased status,
- social popularity,
- human worth,
- general intelligence rankings,
- professional prestige,
- income,
- age,
- educational prestige,
- career seniority,
- activity leaderboards,
- follower counts,
- one isolated assessment,
- self-declared expertise,
- perfect streaks,
- or a public measure of who is "better."

A higher Orbit does not mean:

> better person

It should never imply:

> more valuable human

Orbit progression represents development within GDB's model.

It is not a hierarchy of human worth.


---

## 4. Base GDB Comes First

V2 depends on a useful base product.

The conceptual daily foundation is:

Meaningful intention
→ Focus
→ Completion or interruption
→ Buddy response
→ Consistency evidence
→ Reflection
→ Return
→ repeated experience

Without a valuable daily loop, Orbit progression becomes artificial.

Users should not use GDB merely to increase an Orbit.

The base experience must improve the user's life independently.

A user who never progresses beyond Parallax should still be able to receive a
valuable, satisfying, complete GDB experience.


---

## 5. Parallax Is the Universal Starting Orbit

Parallax is the universal starting environment for the Orbit system.

When a user gains access to Orbits, Parallax should be available immediately.

A user should not need to prove:

- intellectual ability,
- professional experience,
- credentials,
- income,
- age,
- career success,
- or prior achievement

before participating in the GDB Orbit community.

Parallax represents:

- curiosity,
- observation,
- foundational participation,
- the beginning of longitudinal evidence,
- and the beginning of the GDB developmental journey.

Parallax must not feel like a "low-status" location.

An accomplished professional with decades of experience may be in Parallax.

That does not imply that the person is inexperienced, unintelligent, or less
valuable.

It only describes their current position within GDB's developmental system.


---

## 6. No Public Subtiers

Orbits should not contain public internal ranks such as:

- Parallax Level 1,
- Parallax Level 2,
- Parallax Level 3,
- Advanced Parallax,
- Spectroscopy Tier 2,
- percentage progress toward the next Orbit,
- or public cognitive scores.

Creating visible subtiers would recreate the hierarchy problem inside each
Orbit.

GDB may privately accumulate evidence while a user remains within an Orbit.

That private evidence may eventually help the system understand developmental
change.

However:

> internal evidence is not the same thing as public rank.

The user should not feel as though they are grinding XP toward the next Orbit.


---

## 7. Development Is Longitudinal

GDB should prefer patterns over isolated moments.

One excellent focus session does not prove long-term consistency.

One bad day does not prove poor development.

One journal entry does not define a person's thinking.

One assessment should not determine an Orbit.

One impressive answer should not produce immediate progression.

V2 should increasingly reason from evidence accumulated across time.

Conceptually:

Event
→ repeated events
→ patterns
→ trends
→ cautious interpretation
→ developmental reflection
→ possible Orbit recommendation


---

## 8. Evidence Layers

V2 should distinguish different kinds of information.

### Direct Evidence

Things the system directly records.

Examples:

- focus session started,
- focus session completed,
- session duration,
- reflection submitted,
- streak / consistency history,
- selected domain,
- Buddy interactions,
- explicitly stated goals,
- responses to structured prompts.

### Derived Evidence

Information calculated from direct evidence.

Examples:

- consistency trends,
- average focus duration,
- changing work patterns,
- repeated areas of interest,
- completion patterns,
- recurring reflection themes.

### Interpretive Signals

Higher-level hypotheses derived from multiple pieces of evidence.

Examples might eventually include:

- increasing self-awareness,
- greater consistency,
- changing problem-solving approaches,
- stronger systems thinking,
- stronger first-principles reasoning,
- stronger critical reasoning,
- increased ability to synthesize ideas,
- stronger ability to sustain difficult work,
- development within a selected domain.

Interpretive signals must remain probabilistic.

They are not objective truths about the person.


---

## 9. Observation Is Not Interpretation

V2 must distinguish:

> What happened?

from:

> What might it mean?

Example:

Observed:

> User completed 31 focus sessions over eight weeks.

Possible interpretation:

> User may be demonstrating increasing consistency.

Those are not the same statement.

GDB should preserve this distinction throughout the architecture.


---

## 10. Domain Context

Development should be interpreted within context.

A user may select a domain such as:

- software engineering,
- writing,
- entrepreneurship,
- education,
- research,
- design,
- fitness,
- science,
- business,
- or another future domain.

The domain provides context for reflection and development.

GDB should avoid assuming that development in one domain automatically implies
expertise in another.

Domain context should help the system understand how thinking frameworks are
being applied rather than merely what occupation the user holds.


---

## 11. Thinking Development

Long-term V2 may expose users to different thinking frameworks through their
chosen domain.

Potential frameworks include:

- systems thinking,
- first-principles thinking,
- probabilistic thinking,
- scientific thinking,
- economic thinking,
- strategic thinking,
- design thinking,
- computational thinking,
- critical thinking.

Systems thinking may act as a common language throughout GDB.

Other frameworks should be experienced through real problems rather than
treated only as abstract academic tests.

The goal is not to make users perform intelligence.

The goal is to help users develop stronger ways of reasoning.


---

## 12. Orbit Progression Principle

Orbit progression should reflect repeated evidence of developmental depth within
GDB.

It should not be based simply on:

- account age,
- time spent,
- career credentials,
- number of sessions,
- number of reflections,
- longest streak,
- professional success,
- social popularity,
- or money spent.

Conceptually:

Focus
↓
Reflection
↓
Behavior Trends
↓
Thinking Patterns
↓
Longitudinal Evidence
↓
Possible AI Reflection
↓
Orbit Recommendation
↓
Safeguards / Review where appropriate
↓
Possible Orbit Progression

This architecture is intentionally not reduced to:

> If X sessions, then Orbit Y.

Raw activity volume should not automatically produce progression.


---

## 13. Exact Progression Methodology Is TBD

The mechanism used to evaluate Orbit readiness is intentionally undefined.

Future approaches may include:

- structured developmental prompts,
- longitudinal reasoning evidence,
- AI-assisted analysis,
- validated assessments,
- domain-specific challenges,
- behavioral evidence,
- human review where appropriate,
- or a combination of multiple methods.

No progression algorithm should be treated as final before real users provide
evidence that the methodology is:

- useful,
- fair,
- understandable,
- resistant to gaming,
- privacy-conscious,
- and aligned with GDB's values.

The system must earn the right to make developmental interpretations.


---

## 14. Progression Is Not Gamified Leveling

Orbits may use game-like presentation, but the developmental model should not
collapse into XP grinding.

Users should not learn:

> Do 500 sessions and receive Event Horizon.

Users should not see:

> 78% toward Standard Candle.

That would encourage users to optimize for the metric instead of authentic
development.

Progression should reflect evidence, not merely accumulation.


---

## 15. No Single Metric Controls Progression

No one metric should dominate.

Examples of insufficient standalone rules:

- total session count,
- longest streak,
- journal count,
- hours focused,
- one test score,
- AI score,
- Buddy level,
- account age,
- number of social contributions.

Multiple forms of evidence may eventually contribute to a recommendation.


---

## 16. Uncertainty Must Be Allowed

GDB must be able to say:

> I do not know yet.

Possible reasons:

- insufficient evidence,
- contradictory signals,
- limited domain context,
- inconsistent participation,
- low-confidence interpretation,
- unclear reasoning patterns.

The system should not manufacture certainty merely to produce progression.


---

## 17. Orbit Recommendation vs Orbit Judgment

AI may eventually recommend that evidence supports a possible Orbit transition.

A recommendation is not equivalent to an unquestionable judgment.

Conceptually:

Evidence
→ analysis
→ recommendation
→ safeguards
→ possible review
→ progression decision

The architecture may evolve as real-world evidence reveals what degree of human
review is appropriate.

GDB should explain meaningful progression rather than simply announce it.


---

## 18. Human Review

Human review may eventually be useful for higher-impact, ambiguous, or highly
selective progression decisions.

However, human employees should not arbitrarily determine a user's worth or
identity.

Review should evaluate whether the system's developmental conclusion is
supported by available evidence and policy.

The purpose is quality control.

It is not prestige gatekeeping.


---

## 19. Buddy's Role

The Buddy is the emotional and human-facing representation of the GDB
experience.

The Buddy may eventually:

- acknowledge consistency,
- react to completed focus sessions,
- reflect observed patterns,
- ask questions,
- surface changes over time,
- communicate uncertainty,
- celebrate meaningful progress,
- encourage users after setbacks,
- and help explain Orbit recommendations.

The Buddy should never pretend to possess certainty it does not have.

The Buddy supports the mission.

It does not become the mission.


---

## 20. Buddy State and Orbit State Are Different

Buddy state may change frequently.

Examples:

ready
focusing
paused
completed
encouraging
reflective
away

Orbit state should change much more slowly.

The Buddy represents moment-to-moment experience.

Orbits represent longitudinal development.

Do not confuse short-term emotional state with long-term developmental state.


---

## 21. Privacy Boundary

Private reflection data should remain private unless the user explicitly
authorizes another use.

V2 should conceptually separate:

RAW PRIVATE DATA
↓
DERIVED SIGNALS
↓
DEVELOPMENTAL INTERPRETATION
↓
USER-FACING REFLECTION

A user should be able to receive useful developmental insights without GDB
unnecessarily exposing raw personal material.

Administrative access to private journal data should be restricted and
purpose-bound.

Private reflection should not become casual staff-visible content.


---

## 22. User Agency

Users should be able to understand why GDB reaches important developmental
conclusions.

The system should avoid opaque declarations such as:

> You are now a Relativity user because the AI decided.

Where possible, GDB should explain:

- what evidence changed,
- what trends were observed,
- what uncertainty remains,
- and why progression is being suggested.

Developmental interpretation should assist the user rather than define the user
without explanation.


---

## 23. Anti-Gaming Principle

Once progression exists, some users will naturally attempt to optimize for it.

The architecture should therefore make authentic development the easiest
winning strategy.

Possible principles:

- longitudinal evidence over isolated actions,
- quality and context over raw volume,
- multiple signals rather than one metric,
- no public progression formula,
- no purchased advancement,
- no follower or popularity influence,
- no prestige rewards for higher Orbits,
- no public subtiers.

Goodhart's Law should remain an explicit design concern:

> When a measure becomes a target, it can stop being a useful measure.


---

## 24. Cross-Orbit Equality

People in different Orbits should remain socially equal within the broader GDB
ecosystem.

Higher Orbit should NOT automatically grant:

- greater communication rights,
- greater human visibility,
- privileged social access,
- superior matching pools,
- greater social authority,
- or higher social rank.

A person in Parallax may possess knowledge, wisdom, professional experience, or
capabilities that another person in Relativity does not.

Orbit describes development within the GDB model.

It does not comprehensively describe the human.


---

## 25. Cross-Orbit Community

Orbit progression must not produce social segregation.

Users from different Orbits should remain able to:

- communicate,
- collaborate,
- learn from one another,
- exchange ideas,
- participate in shared discussions,
- build friendships,
- and contribute to the broader GDB community.

Orbit membership may shape:

- developmental context,
- identity,
- prompts,
- reflection experiences,
- specialized opportunities,
- or future community experiences.

It should not determine who someone is allowed to speak with.

The intended principle is:

> Progression without segregation.

The GDB community should reward:

- curiosity,
- thoughtfulness,
- creativity,
- respectful disagreement,
- genuine contribution,
- intellectual humility,
- and willingness to learn.

Developmental depth must not become social superiority.


---

## 26. Orbit Interpretations

The following meanings remain conceptual and should continue evolving.

### Parallax

Universal starting environment.

Represents:

- curiosity,
- beginning observation,
- foundational participation,
- creating initial evidence,
- seeing behavior from a new perspective.

Parallax is immediately available to Orbit users.

It is not a remedial or low-status category.

### Standard Candle

Developing clearer reasoning, stronger consistency, and more deliberate
participation.

Early longitudinal patterns become more meaningful.

Progression into Standard Candle should be earned rather than automatic.

### Spectroscopy

Demonstrating deeper analysis, pattern recognition, and evidence-based thinking.

Spectroscopy is intentionally difficult to reach.

A significant portion of GDB may remain in:

- Parallax,
- Standard Candle,
- or Spectroscopy

while still receiving an excellent daily GDB experience.

That is acceptable by design.

### Event Horizon

Sustaining systems-level thinking and engaging meaningfully with complexity.

Evidence increasingly suggests that deeper reasoning patterns are becoming
durable rather than episodic.

### Relativity

Integrating multiple perspectives, systems, context, and cross-domain
reasoning.

The user increasingly recognizes relationships between:

- behavior,
- systems,
- other people,
- context,
- incentives,
- uncertainty,
- and long-term consequences.

### Singularity

An exceptional and highly selective future Orbit.

Its exact entry mechanism is intentionally TBD.

Singularity must never mean:

- smartest human,
- highest intelligence,
- most valuable user,
- highest social class,
- or most successful professional.

Any future implementation requires extremely careful:

- definition,
- governance,
- evidence,
- safeguards,
- and review.


---

## 27. Orbit Transitions Should Be Meaningful

Orbit changes should be relatively rare.

A progression should feel like:

> meaningful evidence has accumulated

rather than:

> another progress bar filled.

Users may remain in an Orbit for:

- months,
- years,
- or indefinitely.

That should not be presented as failure.

Scarcity should emerge from developmental significance, not artificial
friction.


---

## 28. Different Users May Progress at Different Speeds

Orbit progression should not be time-locked.

Two users may join Parallax on the same day and develop differently.

One user may remain in Parallax for a long period.

Another may eventually progress more quickly if their longitudinal evidence
demonstrates stronger developmental depth within GDB.

This difference must not imply:

> one person is better.

The system is observing development within GDB.

It is not producing a universal ranking of people.


---

## 29. No Regression-as-Punishment

Temporary difficulty should not automatically demote users.

Human development is nonlinear.

Possible future architecture may distinguish:

- current activity,
- current consistency,
- long-term evidence,
- Orbit state.

Someone experiencing:

- illness,
- family obligations,
- school pressure,
- work pressure,
- financial difficulty,
- vacation,
- intentional rest,
- or temporary disengagement

should not automatically lose previously demonstrated development.


---

## 31. Relationship to Social Orbits

The Orbit ecosystem may eventually contain significant social/community
functionality.

Users may:

- maintain profiles,
- connect,
- communicate,
- collaborate,
- discover others,
- participate in communities,
- exchange ideas,
- learn across domains.

Social functionality and developmental classification should remain distinct.

An Orbit should never become a caste system.

A healthy community matters more than preserving artificial exclusivity.


---

## 32. Orbit Value Must Exist Without Progression

Users should receive value from their current Orbit.

An Orbit should be valuable to inhabit, not merely valuable to escape.

Higher Orbit placement must not be the only reason to use Orbits.

Potential future Orbit value may include:

- community,
- relevant conversations,
- developmental prompts,
- domain-specific experiences,
- collaboration,
- reflection,
- learning opportunities,
- specialized resources,
- and meaningful identity.

The system should not psychologically pressure users into constant upward
movement.


---

## 33. Orbit Beta Testing

Orbit architecture should be tested independently from the earliest base GDB
beta.

Early GDB testing should first validate:

- focus,
- Buddy,
- consistency,
- reflection,
- return behavior,
- and whether users experience meaningful value.

Later Orbit testing should examine:

- whether users understand Orbit identity,
- whether Parallax feels welcoming,
- whether progression feels meaningful,
- whether progression produces anxiety,
- whether users perceive hierarchy,
- whether cross-Orbit interaction feels healthy,
- whether the community remains respectful,
- whether users attempt to game the system,
- whether Orbit identity adds genuine value.

Orbit testing should be expected to change the architecture.

The current design is a hypothesis.


---

## 34. Relationship to V3

V2 must succeed independently.

Future architecture may depend on V2.

V2 should not depend on that future architecture in order to justify its
existence.

Conceptually:

Base GDB
→ longitudinal evidence
→ useful V2 developmental understanding
→ trust
→ explicit user permission
→ possible future systems

V2 should never be optimized merely to produce future matching or future
platform data.

If later architecture never launches, V2 should still justify its existence.


---

## 35. What V2 Must Prove

Before deeper Orbit intelligence is trusted, GDB should demonstrate that:

1. Users find the base focus/reflection system valuable.
2. Users repeatedly return without artificial coercion.
3. Longitudinal data reveals useful patterns.
4. Users recognize themselves in those patterns often enough to find them
   valuable.
5. AI interpretations can be appropriately bounded.
6. Privacy and consent remain trustworthy.
7. Orbit progression improves the product rather than creating status anxiety.
8. Users cannot easily game the system.
9. Developmental insights produce useful reflection or behavior change.
10. Parallax remains socially legitimate.
11. Cross-Orbit community remains healthy.
12. Higher Orbits do not become prestige classes.


---

## 36. Current Engineering Constraint

Do NOT prematurely build the entire V2 intelligence architecture.

Current priorities remain:

Focus
Buddy
Streaks / Consistency
Reflection
Basic Orbit representation

Do not create unnecessary:

- progression engines,
- AI developmental scoring,
- human-review infrastructure,
- complex Orbit eligibility tables,
- psychological profiles,
- public ranking systems,
- public subtiers,
- progression percentages,
- automated Orbit promotion logic

until product evidence requires them.

Current engineering decisions should preserve the possibility of V2 without
prematurely implementing V2.

The following remain intentionally unresolved:

- How should reasoning depth be evaluated?
- What role should AI play?
- What role should structured prompts play?
- What role should human review play?
- How frequently should Orbit recommendations occur?

- How should domain-specific differences be handled?

## 39. V2 North Star

V2 asks:

> Can repeated evidence of how a person focuses, reflects, learns, struggles,
> adapts, reasons, and acts help that person understand how they are genuinely
> developing over time?

The purpose is not to rank the human.

The purpose is to make development more visible.

And the social purpose is not to separate people by perceived ability.

It is to bring people together around:

- curiosity,
- growth,
- thoughtful reasoning,
- creativity,
- respect,
- and meaningful contribution.

The Orbit system succeeds only if users can develop without feeling socially
diminished by where they currently belong.
